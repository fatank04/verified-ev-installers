# Monetization Strategy — Verified EV Installers

Benchmarks are real (sourced July 2026). Assumptions are labelled as assumptions.
Read the pressure test before the revenue table, not after.

## The three revenue lines, ranked by $/visitor

| # | Line | Unit price | Why it ranks here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Commercial lead routing** | $150-400 per qualified lead | A commercial L2 job is $7k-15k/port and usually multi-port; DCFC is $18k-350k/port. A contractor's gross profit on one job dwarfs any listing fee. Buyers *want* multiple quotes, so the form is the natural path - no need to hide anything. |
| 2 | **Residential lead routing** | $35-60 exclusive | Angi charges $15-85 and Thumbtack $25-75, both **shared with 4-5 pros**. Exclusive is worth more per lead than shared. EV leads convert at 10-20% because the buyer already owns the car. |
| 3 | **Verified/featured listings** | $49-99/mo verified, $149-299/mo featured | Contractor-vertical directories charge $200-400/mo; tiered $49-99 / $149-299 is the standard shape. Sell this **last**, to contractors who already got paid leads from us. |

Deliberately excluded: display ads (destroys trust on a directory whose entire pitch is
independence), and pay-for-placement in rankings (it is the thing we differentiate against,
and it is what Google's site-reputation-abuse policy punishes).

## Pressure test — the ten things that kill this

Ordered by how likely they are to actually kill it.

**1. No traffic means no business, and traffic is 6-12 months out.** Every line above is
downstream of organic search. New domain, zero authority. Nothing else on this list matters
if this one is not solved. *Mitigation: the wedge keywords were chosen precisely because
their SERPs are weak (evitp certified electrician [state], commercial ev charger installation
companies). Long-tail state pages should move in 3-6 months, not 12.*

**2. Selling leads before having leads is impossible; getting contractors before having
traffic is impossible.** Classic cold start. *Mitigation: invert the order. Collect the
sell-side waitlist NOW, while pre-revenue - contractors sign up free to be first in line for
their metro. When leads appear, there is already a buyer list, and the first leads go out
free to prove quality.*

**3. Free-rider problem: we publish every contractor's phone number.** A visitor can skip the
form and call directly. This is real and partly self-inflicted. *Decision: do not hide contact
info - it is the trust proposition and it is what ranks. Instead, monetize where the form
genuinely beats a phone call: commercial buyers who need 3 comparable quotes, not one
electrician. Residential lead capture is a bonus, not the plan.*

**4. Attribution.** Contractors do not pay for leads they cannot verify. *Mitigation: every
lead carries an ID, source URL, metro, and timestamp from day one - built now, before it
matters, so there is a paper trail when the first invoice goes out.*

**5. Lead quality.** One junk lead poisons a contractor relationship faster than ten good ones
build it. *Mitigation: qualification fields on commercial (port count, site type, timeline,
decision role). Fewer, better leads.*

**6. Churn.** Directory churn is brutal when ROI is unclear. *Mitigation: sell subscriptions
only to contractors who already closed a lead from us. Their ROI is already proven at the
point of sale.*

**7. Google dependency.** One core update can halve traffic; thin directories were hit hard
2024-2025 (up to 90% losses). *Mitigation: unique verified data per page, documented
methodology, no scaled thin content. Already built.*

**8. Competitors are moving.** getlocalverified and EVPlugScout both launched EVITP pages in
2026, both thin. *Mitigation: national coverage (2,197 contractors, 50 states) already beats
both. Speed matters more than polish here.*

**9. Payment and legal.** Lead-gen for contractors carries state-level rules; taking money
requires terms and a privacy policy. *Mitigation: terms + privacy shipped before any money
moves. Not legal advice - worth a lawyer's hour before invoicing.*

**10. The data is public.** EVITP publishes these lists. Anyone can scrape them. *Mitigation:
the moat is not the data - it is structure, verification dates, metro assignment, national
coverage, and (eventually) traffic. Data is the entry ticket, not the defense.*

## Revenue model — three scenarios

Traffic is the input everything else derives from. Conversion assumptions are stated.

Assumptions: 2% of visitors start a quote request; 20% of those are commercial; commercial
lead sells at $250, residential at $45; listing conversion applies to the 2,197 contractors at
the standard 2-5% basic / 0.5-1% premium rates.

| | Bear (mo 12) | Base (mo 12) | Bull (mo 12) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Organic sessions/mo | 1,500 | 5,000 | 15,000 |
| Quote requests (2%) | 30 | 100 | 300 |
| Commercial (20%) | 6 | 20 | 60 |
| Commercial revenue | $1,500 | $5,000 | $15,000 |
| Residential revenue | $1,080 | $3,600 | $10,800 |
| Listings (2%/0.5% @ $79/$199) | $0 (too early) | $1,700 | $3,500 |
| **Monthly total** | **$2,580** | **$10,300** | **$29,300** |

The bear case still covers the domain, hosting ($0), and time. The base case is a real side
business. Note how little of this comes from listings - that line only works after traffic.

Costs: domain ~$12/yr, Render static $0, form backend $0, refresh automation $0. Gross margin
is effectively 100% until a lead CRM or paid tooling is added.

## Sequencing — what happens when

**Phase 0 (now, pre-traffic).** No charging. Instrument everything: lead IDs, source
attribution, analytics. Publish pricing openly so contractors self-select. Collect the
sell-side waitlist. Ship terms + privacy. **This phase is what gets executed today.**

**Phase 1 (first organic traffic, ~mo 3-6).** Route commercial leads free to waitlist
contractors. Collect outcomes ("did this close?"). That data is the sales asset for Phase 2.

**Phase 2 (proven leads, ~mo 6-9).** Start charging per commercial lead. Founding-partner
pricing for waitlist members. Stripe payment links - no billing system needed at this volume.

**Phase 3 (~mo 9-12+).** Verified/featured subscriptions, sold only to contractors with a
closed lead. Then O&M/maintenance vertical (Tier 3 keywords, already mapped, near-zero SERP
competition).

## What would make me abandon this

Stated up front so it is not rationalized away later:
- Wedge pages not ranking top-20 for their state term by month 6, with content and links tried.
- Commercial quote requests under 3/month once traffic exceeds 3,000 sessions/month (means the
  funnel premise is wrong, not just small).
- Fewer than 20 contractors on the sell-side waitlist after 200 outreach contacts (means
  contractors do not want leads badly enough to pay).
